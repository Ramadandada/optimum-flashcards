## 3. COLD CALLING — The 7-Step Door

You walk in with **a pen and a smile**. Nothing else. No bag, no folder, no laptop.

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Greeting** — "Hey, lovely place." |
| 2 | **Place yourself in area** — "I have an appointment next door at ABC, I had a few minutes…" |
| 3 | **Ask the magic question** — "Who can I contact about what you do for drinking water and ice for your employees?" |
| 4 | **Get the card** — "Does Bobby have a business card I could grab?" |
| 5 | **Gather info** — "Real quick, what do you guys do for water? Five gallon jugs? How many coolers? Any issues with the water? Do you guys do anything for ice?" |
| 6 | **Get the gatekeeper's name** — "What was your name?" |
| 7 | **Get out** — "Awesome, thanks for your help." |

### Why "who can I contact" works
- The phrase **disarms the gatekeeper** — "contact" is future tense, signals you're leaving immediately
- Placing yourself in the area makes you feel **targeted, not random**
- Never pitch at the door — you're **75% less likely to close** if they don't invite you in

### Card classification
Rate each card 1–5. Call the 3s, 4s, 5s yourself. Pass the 1s and 2s to the telemarketer.

## 4. PHONE — The 4-Step Appointment Set

Total call: **30 seconds**. That's it.

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| 1. Greeting / clear time | "Hey JP, this is Paul with Optimum. Glad I caught you, I'll be quick." |
| 2. Relevance | "I stopped by, talked to Selina up front. She mentioned you're using 5-gallon jugs and running out, and the machines are getting kind of nasty." |
| 3. Value proposition | "We have systems that give you a never-ending supply of purified water, usually cost less than the jugs, and they're the most sanitary on the market. We work with a bunch of your neighbors." |
| 4. Assumptive close | "I'm gonna be next door Tuesday and Thursday — does Tuesday at 2 work, or is Thursday better?" |

### Objection → bounce back to value prop → reclose
- **"We're all set"** → "Sally said you don't actually drink it. Most of your neighbors said the same until they saw what we do. Tuesday or Thursday?"
- **"We tried it before"** → "You haven't tried ours. Have you ever had Smart Water? Our machines make Smart Water. Just 10 minutes — Tuesday or Thursday?"
- **"Send info"** → "If I email you'll have more questions than answers. That's why I do everything in person — only 10 minutes. Tuesday or Thursday?"

**Never ask "is now a good time?"** — it's a yes/no that invites a no. Just clear the time with a statement.

## 5. THE 10-STEP PITCH — Memorize This Order

This is the spine. Every appointment runs these in order.

| # | Step | Core move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Greeting** | "Thanks for taking time. Beautiful office. Somewhere we can sit?" |
| 2 | **Build rapport** | Ask about them. How long here? Where from? Family pics? Find common ground. |
| 3 | **Gather info** | "Before I show you anything, let me ask a few questions." |
| 4 | **Go to the cooler** | "Mind if we take a look at your system?" |
| 5 | **Destroy what they have** | Open system, growth, cross-contamination, dog bowl, 5 gallon jug back injuries |
| 6 | **Describe our units top to bottom** | I-14 first, then PW-90 — never list everything |
| 7 | **Purification process** | RO + boost filter + in-tank sanitation |
| 8 | **Installation** | "Like a fridge ice maker. Lines run like cable." |
| 9 | **Service** | "Maintenance schedule we manage for you." |
| 10 | **Close for the demo** | "If you were to do a free trial, would this be the spot? Which machine would you like to try?" |

### Step 3 — Gather Info questions
- How long providing this water solution? Why?
- How many employees? How many drinking water stations?
- Issues with delivery or service? How fast does service come?
- What do you spend monthly/yearly?
- Do you do anything for ice?
- Who cleans the coolers? When was the last cleaning?
- Have you had any taste issues?

### Step 5 — Destroying their system

**If they have 5-gallon jugs:**
- "This is an open system. Dust on the bottle goes right in the tank."
- "Hear that *glug glug*? That's outside air being pulled in through the opposite spigot. Whatever's in the air, on people's hands, on bottles — pulled into the tank."
- **The dog bowl line:** "Do you have a dog at home? You fill the bowl, two days later it's slimy. That's organic compounds reacting with plastic. Same thing in this tank."
- "International Bottled Water Association says clean every 6 weeks. Nobody does."
- "Cross-contamination — hands all over the lever, sport bottles jammed against spigots people drank from for days. Viruses live on plastic 3–5 days. **This is how workplace epidemics happen.**"
- "Who's the lucky person changing 42-pound bottles? **60% of office workman's comp claims are from these jugs.** Average non-surgical back claim is ~$100,000."

**If they have a filter cooler:**
- "Carbon filter only removes gases — chlorine, sulfur. Same as your fridge or a Brita."
- "Lead, mercury, arsenic, chromium 6 all stay in your water."
- **Run TDS test on their cooler vs. tap** — usually identical. "You're drinking the same as tap, except now it sat in a tank where bacteria grew because chlorine got removed."
- "When did you last clean the tank?"
- **Run the precipitator demo** — electrical current pulls TDS out of solution. Hand them their water, hold yours. *"Cheers. Which would you rather drink?"*

### Step 6 — Pitch only TWO machines

Lead with the **I-14** (ice + water) — even if they say no ice initially.

> "Our I-14 is our best seller. 17 inches wide, 13 pounds of ice ready, 45 lbs/day, hot/cold/room temp water, more than 5 gallons at the ready. Touch-free. 12-inch dispense area for big mugs. Active drain so you never empty a tray. Stainless tank — bacteria can't grow."

Then the **PW-90** (water only).

> "Same size as your current cooler, way more ergonomic — chest level sensor, table-height dispense. Hot at 85°C for tea/oatmeal/soup, cold at 3°C."

### Step 7 — Purification (memorize this exact sequence)

> "Are you familiar with reverse osmosis? **Known worldwide as the best way to purify drinking water.** All our systems are RO. **Have you ever had Smart Water? Our machines make Smart Water** — that's electrolyte-enhanced, pH-balanced water. We use a boost filter that adds calcium, magnesium, potassium, sodium bicarbonate. Then we sanitize in-tank with activated oxygen or LED UV. Nothing organic can grow. We work with hospitals, dialysis clinics, senior living — most sanitary systems on the market."

### Step 8 — Installation script

> "We hook up like a fridge ice maker. Our lines look like cable wire — we run them up walls, over drop ceilings. Under the sink: turn off cold water valve, unscrew line to sink, put a brass T in. Reconnect to sink, our tube goes to the machine. Small hole behind the cooler covered with a light switch plate. **Takes my guy 20–30 minutes. 100% reversible.**"

### Step 9 — Service script

> "We put you on a maintenance schedule we manage for you. Once a year we change all your filters no matter what — **rated for 1,500 gallons, that's a lot of water.** There's a microprocessor monitoring filter life AND water quality. If anything's off in between, the machine tells you. **You never drink or pay for imperfect water.** Call me directly — never an 800 number. **If we don't fix an issue in 5 days, you don't pay for that month.**"

### Step 10 — Close for demo (3 micro-steps)

1. Make them feel special — "Because you're a major / because we work with so many of your neighbors, we offer a free trial."
2. **"If you were to do a free trial, would this be the best spot — or somewhere else?"**
3. **"Which machine would you like to try?"** *(never "do you want one" — that's yes/no)*

Take **3 photos** before you leave: where the cooler will go, under the sink, and a wide shot showing the path between them.

## 7. WATER SCIENCE — Enough to Sound Smart

### TDS chart (memorize the buckets)
- **<20 PPM** = pure / RO water
- **<50 PPM** = high-quality bottled
- **80–450 PPM** = municipal tap
- **170+ PPM** = hard water
- **300+ PPM** = high TDS (don't drink)
- **500 PPM** = max contaminant level

### What's in tap water
- **IOCs** (inorganic — non-living): lead, mercury, arsenic, chromium 6, fluoride, nitrate, nitrite, copper
- **VOCs** (organic — living/chemical): solvents, herbicides, pesticides — *carbon filtration removes these*
- **PFAs** ("forever chemicals") — never leave your body, linked to chronic illness/cancer
- **Pharmaceuticals** — flushed drugs in the water supply

### Filtration vs. Purification
- **Filtration** = carbon, microfiltration. Removes gases (chlorine, sulfur), some particles.
- **Purification** = reverse osmosis. Strips everything down to 0.0001 micron.

### Why pH matters
- Pure water becomes **acidic** (~5.5–6) after RO
- Acidic water in the body = more susceptible to disease
- Boost filter → **alkaline** = healthier
- This is why "Smart Water" sells

### In-tank sanitation methods
| Method | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Activated Oxygen (O3)** | Bubbles oxygen through tank, kills 100% of bacteria/mold/slime/viruses | Never loses effectiveness, not affected by TDS | Can give metallic taste if set too high |
| **LED UV** | Light wavelengths attack DNA of microorganisms | 8-year bulb life, no dimming | More expensive |
| **Silver ion** | Ions kill bacteria on contact | Used on our machine *exterior* coating | Loses charge over time inside tanks |
| ❌ Traditional UV bulb | Gets covered in algae and dims | — | Becomes a heat source for bacteria — *opposite effect* |

### "Follow the Pen" — the ritual close
On Page 2, point as you say each thing in this order:
1. Customer info → here
2. Price → "$115 a month"
3. Billing frequency → "We'll bill you monthly"
4. **Term** → "We guarantee your price for 60 months" *(sandwich it)*
5. Install fee → "And there's a one-time $275 install fee. **Sign there. Flip the page when you're done.**"

**Sandwich the term between price and install fee.** If they're going to push back, it'll be on the install fee (the last thing you said) — easier to handle than fighting term.

### Term objection → "60 months is our standard term." Then **shut up.**

If they push:
- "These machines are expensive — we typically don't break even until 30–36 months. That's why the term."
- "I'll waive the install fee at 60 months."
- "I'll give you a free month at 60 months."
- Last resort: "I can do 36 — let me make a quick call." *(call your manager, fake-negotiate)*

### The sneaky Page 3 trick
On a multi-unit deal where they're sign-and-flipping mindlessly:
> "This is delivery and acceptance — **just a permission slip for me to install the rest of your machines.** Sign there."
*(Not technically true, but you can't submit for funding until all units are installed anyway, so no harm done — you just save yourself a second trip.)*

### After they sign — ASK FOR THE REFERRAL

## 13. THE INTANGIBLES — Mindset

- **People buy from people.** Steps 1–3 of the pitch (greeting, rapport, gather info) are not optional. New reps skip them and lose deals.
- **Don't pitch at the door.** 75% lower close rate. Set the appointment, get invited in.
- **Place yourself in the area** — every cold call, every phone intro. Disarms the human filter.
- **Volume of accounts > genius selling.** Hit 20 doors a day, the rest follows.
- **Don't sell on price** — sell on value, problems, hassles. Save price discussions for when you must.
- **Always be assumptive.** "Which machine would you like to try?" not "Do you want a free trial?" Don't ask yes/no questions you don't want a no to.
- **Always set the next step on YOUR schedule.** "I'll be in your area Tuesday at 2" — not "Call me when you're ready." Customers will drag deals out forever if you let them.
- **Two-day follow-up in person** — not phone. The face-to-face triples your close rate.
- **Pre-fill paperwork.** Print blank agreements with your name and X marks where signatures go. Save yourself hours.
- **Use TurboScan** (or notes app) on your phone to scan & email contracts. Pays for itself once.
- **Take 3 photos** every free trial: placement spot, water source, wide shot.
